# Tank Siege

A modern take on the classic top-down tank game (Battle City), made with **Godot 4.5** and **GDScript**. Defend the core, destroy 20 tanks per stage, survive the bosses, and build a stronger tank as you go.

All the pixel art, sound effects and chiptune music are original. They're generated by small Python scripts in `tools/`, so you can tweak them and regenerate them.

- **Play it:** download the newest version for Windows or macOS from the [website](https://foxsoft2005.github.io/tank-siege/) or the [Releases page](https://github.com/foxsoft2005/tank-siege/releases/latest).
- **Features:** local 2-player co-op, 3 difficulty levels, 8 enemy types with pathfinding AI, 2 bosses, roguelite upgrade cards, a Garage with unlockable tanks and perks, 33 achievements, a stats screen after every stage, procedural levels, a level editor, special tiles (ice, mud, belts, teleporters, explosive barrels), secret codes and automatic updates.

# 1. Playing the game

## Install

| | Windows | macOS |
|---|---|---|
| File | `TankSiege-windows.zip` | `TankSiege-macos.zip` |
| Needs | Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit | macOS 11 or newer (Apple Silicon or Intel) |
| Install | Unzip, then run `TankSiege.exe` | Unzip, then move **Tank Siege** to Applications |

The game isn't signed with a paid certificate, so the first launch shows a warning:
- **Windows**, "Windows protected your PC": click **More info**, then **Run anyway**.
- **macOS**: right-click the app, choose **Open**, then **Open** again. If macOS says the app "is damaged", run this in Terminal:
  `xattr -cr "/Applications/Tank Siege.app"`

The game checks for updates on the title screen and can install most of them for you (see [Updates](#updates)).

## Goal and rules

- Protect the **glowing core** at the bottom of the map. If a shell hits it, the game is over.
- Each stage sends **20 enemy tanks**, with only a few on screen at once. Destroy them all to clear the stage.
- You have a few **lives**. When you run out, the game is over.
- After each stage there's a **stats screen**, then you pick an **upgrade card**, then the next stage starts.
- Every **5th stage** is a **boss** fight.
- There are 4 handmade stage maps, which loop, plus the boss arena. You can also turn on **procedural levels** for a new random map every stage.

## Enemies

| Tank | Color | From stage | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basic | grey | 1 | The classic |
| Fast | mint | 1 | Quick, and hunts you |
| Power | purple | 1 | Fires fast shells |
| Armored | green | 1 | Takes 4 hits, and glows hotter as it's damaged |
| **Kamikaze** | orange | 2 | Never shoots. It rushes you, lights a fuse and **explodes**, hurting everything nearby, including other enemies. It also blows up walls in its way. Shoot it early, ideally next to its friends! |
| **Sniper** | navy | 3 | When it lines up with you, it shows a **red laser sight**, then fires a very fast shell. Break the line or dodge. |
| **Shielded** | teal | 4 | A steel plate **deflects shells from the front**. Hit it from the side or behind. Explosions ignore the shield. |
| **Engineer** | khaki | 4 | Drives to bricks you've shot away and **rebuilds** them. Destroy it first. |

**Enemies think.** Some tanks wander like in the original. **Raiders** find a route to your core and shoot through walls to get there. **Hunters** chase you and re-plan as you move. Later stages send more of the smart ones.

**Enemies need time to react.** An enemy can't spin round and shoot you the moment you line up with it. It has to notice you, turn (you can see it rotate) and steady its gun first. That's about 1 second for a tank facing away from you on Normal, so you can sneak up behind one. While it's getting ready, the **tip of its barrel glows orange**: that's your cue to shoot first or dodge.

Enemies **#4, #11 and #18** of each stage blink red. Destroy one and it drops a **power-up** somewhere you can reach.

## Bosses

Every 5th stage is a boss fight. A siren sounds, the boss music starts, a health bar appears, and escorts come with the boss. Destroying it clears the stage and drops 2 power-ups. The two bosses take turns, and each gets tougher every time you meet it ("MK-2", "MK-3"…).

- **Fortress** (stages 5, 15, 25…): a huge twin-cannon tank. It fires spreads of shells, then aimed shots and escorts, and finally **enrages**: it glows red, moves lower and faster, and fires rings of 8 shells. A steel bunker above your core stops its straight-down shots.
- **Sky Raider** (stages 10, 20, 30…): a helicopter gunship that **flies over walls and water**, so you can't hide. It fires aimed bursts, and before each **strafing run** it flashes a **red warning stripe** across one row. Get out of the stripe! It enrages too.

Freeze doesn't stop a boss, and a Bomb only damages it.

## Your tank: stars and armor

As in the original, each star changes your tank's **shape**, not just its stats. Every star also adds **one armor plate**: one more hit you can survive.

| Stars | Look | Effect | Hits to destroy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | light tank, short gun | | 1 |
| 1 | long gun | faster shells | 2 |
| 2 | bigger turret, twin guns | 2 shells on screen at once | 3 |
| 3 | heavy tank with armor plates | shells break steel | 4 |

The small grey plates on your treads show your armor, and a plate that's been shot off turns dark. After a hit you blink for a second, so you can get away. When you lose a life, you also lose your stars, except on Easy.

A gold arrow on the back always marks *your* tank (green for Player 2).

## Combo

Kills less than **2.5 seconds** apart build a **combo**. Each kill in the chain is worth its points × the chain length, up to **×8**. The sidebar shows the multiplier and a bar that drains until the chain breaks, and chains of 3, 5, 8 and 12 get a shout: *NICE! GREAT! UNSTOPPABLE! LEGENDARY!*

- **The numbers**: time, shells fired, accuracy, best combo, walls smashed, power-ups, enemy shells shot down, armor lost and score.
- **Awards**: score bonuses for playing well.
- **Achievements** you unlocked during the stage.

| Award | For | Bonus |
|---|---|---|

| Sharpshooter | 75%+ of your shells hitting | +500 |
| Blitz | clearing the stage in under 1:30 | +500 |

You also see a new high score, if you set one, and the scrap you earned. Choose **Retry** or **Main menu** (Esc also goes to the menu).

| Bulwark | 80 | Survives an extra hit every life, but slow |

| Perk | Cost | Effect |
|---|---|---|

## Achievements

There are **33 achievements** in bronze, silver and gold. When you unlock one, a popup slides in with a fanfare. **Title → Achievements** lists them all, with progress for the counting ones ("37 / 100"). A few are **secret** and stay hidden until you find them.

A few examples:
- *Eagle Eye*: destroy a Sniper while its laser is on you.
- *Hot Potato*: shoot a Kamikaze after its fuse is lit.
- *Bomb Squad*: destroy 4 tanks with one Bomb.
- *LEGENDARY!*: chain 12 kills in one combo.
- *Tiptoe*: clear a stage without breaking a single wall.
- *Ammo Miser*: clear a stage firing fewer than 40 shells.
- *Never Give Up*: lose 50 tanks.

Runs with cheats don't unlock achievements (apart from two secret ones about cheating), and custom levels only count toward *Architect*.

## Co-op

**Title → Co-op (2 players)**. Both players share one screen and one keyboard, or use two gamepads.

| | Player 1 (gold arrow) | Player 2 (green arrow) |
|---|---|---|
| Drive | W A S D | Arrow keys |
| Fire | Space or J | Enter, Right Ctrl or Numpad 0 |
| Gamepad | first gamepad | second gamepad |

- Each player has **their own lives and star level**. Stars, shields and extra lives go to whoever picks them up.
- **Score, combo and upgrade cards are shared.**
- When one player is out of lives, the other fights on. It's game over when both are out, or when the core falls.
- Enemies go after whichever player is **closest**, and one extra enemy is allowed on screen.
- Player 1 drives the tank chosen in the Garage. Player 2 drives a Scout.

## Procedural levels

Turn on **Options → Procedural levels** and every stage becomes a new random map. It's **off by default**, which gives the handmade stages, and it takes effect from the next stage. Boss stages keep their arena.

Every generated map is checked before you play it:
- every enemy spawn can reach your base and your spawn;
- there are no sealed-off areas;
- belts, barrels and teleporters are placed safely;
- there's always steel between the top-middle spawn and your base, so an enemy can't shoot the base right after it appears.

Special tiles unlock stage by stage, as in the handmade maps. A stage keeps its map if you replay it in the same run, and a new run brings new maps.

With **Check for updates** on, the title screen shows "Update vX.Y.Z available!" when there's a newer version.
- **Download & install** downloads the update in the game, checks it isn't damaged, and asks you to restart.
- **Open download page** opens the website. Some updates, such as bigger changes to the game engine settings, need a full new download, and then this is the only button.

Your progress is kept either way.

## Secret codes (spoilers!)

Type these during a stage or in the pause menu. There's no text box: just type. A sparkle sound and a message confirm the code worked.

| Code | Effect |
|---|---|
| `GODTIER` | God mode on/off: nothing can destroy your tank. Your core can still fall! |
| ↑ ↑ ↓ ↓ ← → ← → B A | +30 lives (works on a gamepad too) |
| `OVERLOAD` | Max stars: fast shells, 2 at once, breaks steel, full armor |
| `BLASTOFF` | Destroy every enemy on screen |
| `CASTLE` | Steel walls around the core for the rest of the stage, plus 3 core shield charges |
| `SHORTCUT` | Skip the stage (you still get your upgrade card) |
| `LOOTBOX` | 3 free random upgrades |
| `DISCO` | Rainbow tanks on/off |

Codes are read by key position, so they work with any keyboard layout. Using a code turns off high scores, scrap and achievements for that run (the game-over screen says so). God mode and disco stay on through a restart until you type the code again.

## Where your progress is saved

Everything is saved on your computer, in the game's data folder:

| System | Folder |
|---|---|
| Windows | `%APPDATA%\Godot\app_userdata\Tank Siege\` |
| macOS | `~/Library/Application Support/Godot/app_userdata/Tank Siege/` |

It contains:
- `save.cfg`: high scores, scrap, Garage, achievements and lifetime stats
- `settings.cfg`: options
- `levels/`: your custom levels
- `updates/`: downloaded updates

Delete the folder to start completely fresh. Delete just `updates/` to go back to the version you installed.

# 2. Development

## Run from source

1. Install **Godot 4.5** (the standard version, not .NET) from [godotengine.org](https://godotengine.org).
2. In the Project Manager, click **Import**, choose `project.godot` from this folder, then **Import & Edit**.
3. Press **F5** (or ▶, top right). The game starts at the title screen.

Extra keys when running from the editor:
- **F2** clears the stage instantly, which is handy for testing the stats screen and upgrade cards.
- **F3** shows the enemy AI's planned routes: yellow for raiders, red for hunters.

The asset generators in `tools/` need Python 3:
- `make_sprites.py` needs Pillow.
- `make_sfx.py` needs numpy.
- `make_music.py` needs numpy and ffmpeg.

## How it works

### Scenes, autoloads and the game loop

There are four scenes: title, game, editor and Garage. They switch with `get_tree().change_scene_to_file()`. The **autoloads** survive every scene change, so the run, the music and the sound carry on. `GameState` holds the current run (stage, lives, stars, score, upgrade cards and stats) and the saved progress.

A stage is one load of `main.tscn`. `main.gd` builds the level, spawns the players and enemies, and runs the HUD. When the stage is cleared:
1. It shows the `StatsScreen`.
2. It shows the `UpgradeScreen`.
3. It increases `GameState.stage` and calls `reload_current_scene()`.

At game over it freezes every tank (`_freeze_battlefield()`), then shows the stats screen with Retry and Main menu buttons.

Pausing uses `get_tree().paused`. The world is pausable, while `Main` and the menus keep running. **Hit-stop** (the tiny freeze on each kill) sets `Engine.time_scale` to 0.05 and ends on the real clock.

### Maps and tiles

A map is 13×13 **blocks** of 32 px, one character per block (see [Map format](#map-format)). `Level.build()` turns each block into four 16 px wall pieces, so a shell can break half a block, like the original.

Ice, mud, belts and teleporters are **floor tiles** with no collision. `Level.floor_under()` tells a tank what it's standing on, and `Tank.drive()` applies the effect. Water sits on its own physics layer, so tanks hit it but shells fly over. The layers are listed in `layers.gd`.

### Map format

| Char | Tile | Char | Tile |
|---|---|---|---|
| `.` | empty | `I` | ice |
| `B` | brick | `M` | mud |
| `S` | steel | `^ V < >` | conveyor belt (direction) |
| `W` | water | `T` | teleporter |
| `G` | bushes | `X` | explosive barrel |

The enemy spawns (top-left, top-middle and top-right), the two player spawns and the core with its wall ring must stay empty. They're listed in `LevelData.PROTECTED`, and `LevelData.sanitize()` enforces them.

### Enemy AI

Each enemy gets a **brain** when it spawns:
- **Wander**: random turns, drifting toward the base.
- **Raider**: plans a route to a spot next to the core, then attacks it.
- **Hunter**: plans a route to the nearest player and re-plans as they move.

`Level` keeps an `AStarGrid2D` (Godot's built-in A\*) with a point for every place a tank can stand:
- Steel, water and the core are solid.
- Bricks are walkable but expensive, so raiders take open roads unless blasting through a wall is much shorter.

**Reaction times:** before firing, an enemy has to notice you, turn and aim. The constants are at the top of `enemy.gd` (`REACTION`, `TURN_90`, `TURN_180` and `AIM_TIME`), scaled by the difficulty's `enemy_reaction`.

**Special enemies** are subclasses of `Enemy` that override a few hooks (`_think`, `_configure`, `_path_goal`, `hit`…). `Enemy.create(kind)` builds the right one.

### Procedural levels

`level_gen.gd` builds a map in four steps:
1. **Stamp:** random wall pieces go on the left half.
2. **Mirror:** the left half is copied onto the right.
3. **Bunker:** a small steel bunker goes in the base's column.
4. **Validate:** the map is checked with flood fills over the block grid.

The validation checks:
- the protected blocks are clear;
- every enemy spawn can reach the base and the player spawns (steel, water and barrels block, bricks don't);
- there are no unreachable pockets;
- the brick, steel and open-space counts are within limits;
- belts never push into solid tiles;
- barrels aren't next to a spawn or the base;
- teleporters come in pairs;
- there's steel between the top-middle spawn and the base.

A map that fails is regenerated. In testing, 3,000 maps across stages 1–30 needed about 2 tries each and under 1 ms. The seed is `hash([GameState.run_seed, stage])`, so a stage keeps its map within a run. `GameState.current_map()` decides between a custom level, a procedural map and a handmade map.

### Power-ups

`Level.powerup_spots()` lists the blocks where a power-up may land:
- completely empty;
- not under a bush;
- not on a belt or teleporter;
- not next to the core;
- **reachable** from the player (flood fill over the navigation grid).

`Main` picks one, avoiding spots where a power-up already is.

### Stats and achievements

`GameState.track(key, amount, player)` counts events as they happen. Each counter goes into three dictionaries:
- `stage_stats`: this stage
- `run_stats`: the whole run
- `lifetime`: saved, and used by achievements

Kills know who made them because each shell stamps `tank.last_hit_by` before it hits. Counter achievements unlock themselves when a lifetime counter reaches its goal. The others call `Achievements.unlock("id")`.

### Saving

`GameState.save_progress()` writes `user://save.cfg`, and `Settings` writes `user://settings.cfg`, both with `ConfigFile`. Custom levels are text files in `user://levels/`.

### UI

Menus are built in code with `MenuKit` helpers (buttons, sliders, toggles and the shared Options page) and styled by one `Theme` from `ui_theme.gd`. Buttons get mouse, keyboard and gamepad support for free through Godot's focus system.

### Art, sound and music

- **Art:** sprites are tiny (tanks are 16×16), drawn at 2× with **Nearest** filtering so the pixels stay sharp. `tools/make_sprites.py` draws them from text grids and a few numbers per tank shape.
- **Sound effects:** synthesized by `tools/make_sfx.py`, from oscillators with pitch sweeps and envelopes, like sfxr.
- **Music:** `tools/make_music.py` writes four chiptune loops (two battle themes, a menu theme and a boss theme) from note lists.
- **Sound playback:** `Sfx` plays sounds from a small pool of players, with a little random pitch.
- **Music playback:** `Music` crossfades between tracks and muffles them while the game is paused.

### Secret codes

`cheat_codes.gd` keeps the last 16 key and gamepad presses. It checks whether they end with a code, then emits `code_entered(id)`. `Main._on_cheat()` applies the effect and sets `GameState.cheats_used`.

| **A campaign stage** | Build it in the level editor, click **Copy**, and paste it into `MAPS` in `scripts/level_data.gd`. It becomes the next stage in the loop. |
| **An upgrade card** | Add an entry to `ALL` in `upgrades.gd`, then read its level with `GameState.stacks("id")` where it matters (e.g. `player.gd`). |
| **An enemy type** | Copy one of `scripts/enemies/`, override the hooks you need, and add it to `Enemy.Kind`, `Enemy.create()` and `Main._pick_enemy_kind()` (which stage it appears from, and how often). For the stats tally, add a row to `StatsScreen.ROWS`. |
| **An achievement** | Add a line to `LIST` in `achievements.gd`. Give it a `"stat"` and `"goal"` (any `GameState.track()` key), or call `Achievements.unlock("id")` where it happens. |
| **A stage award** | Add a condition to `Main._stage_medals()`. |
| **A Garage tank or perk** | Add it to `garage.gd`. A tank is a dictionary of stat changes, and a perk is read with `GameState.perk("id")`. For a new tank color, add a palette line in `tools/make_sprites.py` and rerun it. |
| **A secret code** | Add it to `CODES` in `cheat_codes.gd` and a branch in `Main._on_cheat()`. Avoid the letters P, M, N and J, which already do things. |
| **A sound** | Drop a `.wav` in `assets/sfx/`, add its name to `SOUND_NAMES` in `sfx.gd`, then call `Sfx.play("name")`. |
| **Music** | Replace an `.ogg` in `assets/music/`, keeping the name, or edit a song in `tools/make_music.py`. |
| **Balance changes** | Difficulty numbers are in `difficulty.gd`, combo settings (`COMBO_WINDOW`, `MAX_MULTIPLIER`) in `main.gd`, boss health in `boss.gd` and `gunship.gd`, and prices in `garage.gd`. |

## Building and releasing

### Automatically on GitHub

`.github/workflows/build.yml` builds the game on GitHub's Linux machines. Godot can export Windows and macOS games from Linux, and one build takes 1–2 minutes. To publish a version:

1. Set the same version in `scripts/version.gd` (`VERSION`) and `project.godot` (`config/version`).
2. Commit, then tag and push:
   ```
   git tag v1.6.1
   git push origin main v1.6.1
   ```
3. A GitHub Release appears with:
   - `TankSiege-windows.zip`: a single `.exe`, plus the licenses
   - `TankSiege-macos.zip`: a universal `.app`, plus the licenses
   - `tank-siege.pck` and `update.json`: for the in-game updater

The zip names never change, so `https://github.com/foxsoft2005/tank-siege/releases/latest/download/TankSiege-windows.zip` always gets the newest version. The build stops with an error if the tag and the version numbers don't match.

To try a build without making a release, open **Actions → Build game → Run workflow**. The zips appear under **Artifacts** on that run's page.

### By hand in Godot

1. **Editor → Manage Export Templates → Download and Install** (once).
2. **Project → Export**, where the *Windows Desktop* and *macOS* presets are ready. Click **Export Project…** with **Export With Debug** off.

Export settings that matter:
- Windows embeds the game data in the `.exe`.
- macOS is a universal build with bundle id `com.foxsoft2005.tanksiege` and free "ad-hoc" signing.
- `project.godot` enables *Import ETC2 ASTC*, which Godot needs for Apple Silicon.

Removing the macOS warning needs an Apple Developer account ($99/year): fill in the preset's *Codesign* and *Notarization* options.

## Auto-update

`scripts/updater.gd`:
1. **Check:** it downloads `update.json` from `MANIFEST_URL`, which is `https://github.com/foxsoft2005/tank-siege/releases/latest/download/update.json`.
2. **Compare:** it compares versions number by number, so 1.10 is newer than 1.9.
3. **Download:** it downloads the `.pck` patch and verifies its **SHA-256**.
4. **Install:** it saves the patch to `user://updates/`. On the next launch, `Updater` (the first autoload) mounts it before anything else loads.

Only `https://` URLs are allowed. The patch is re-checked on every launch. A patch older than the installed game is deleted.

**Patch or full download:** a patch can't change `project.godot` or add or rename `class_name` scripts. The build script compares each release with the previous tag:
- If neither changed, `update.json` offers the in-game patch.
- If either changed, it only offers **Open download page** (the website).

You don't have to decide anything: tag and push.

**Testing updates locally:** run `python -m http.server 8765` in a folder containing a `.pck` and an `update.json` that use `http://localhost:8765/...` URLs. Then start the game with:
```
godot --path . -- --test-updates --manifest=http://localhost:8765/update.json
```
Patches are ignored when running from the editor without `--test-updates`, so a patch can never hide your source files.

The updater only works for players on 1.5.1 or newer (the first version with `MANIFEST_URL`), and only while the repository is public.

## Website

`site/` is a one-page website (`index.html` and `img/`) in the game's style, with Windows and macOS download buttons that always point at the newest release. `.github/workflows/pages.yml` publishes it to **https://foxsoft2005.github.io/tank-siege/** whenever something in `site/` changes, or by hand from **Actions → Deploy website**. One-time setup: **Settings → Pages → Source: GitHub Actions**.

`site/.gdignore` makes Godot skip the folder, so the website images aren't imported or packed into the game.

## Godot ideas for a C# / Python programmer

- **Nodes and the scene tree.** A game is a tree of nodes, each doing one job:
  - `Node2D`: has a position.
  - `CharacterBody2D`: moves and collides.
  - `Area2D`: detects overlaps.
  - `Label`: shows text.

  `add_child()` adds a node to the game, and `queue_free()` deletes it at the end of the frame.
- **Scripts extend node types.** `extends CharacterBody2D` is like `class Tank : CharacterBody2D`. `class_name Tank` makes it usable everywhere by name, and `super(delta)` calls the parent's method.
- **Callbacks**, like Unity's `Update`:
  - `_ready()` runs once, when the node enters the game.
  - `_process(delta)` runs every frame.
  - `_physics_process(delta)` runs at a fixed 60 Hz.
  - `_draw()` draws shapes; call `queue_redraw()` to repeat it.
- **Signals** are C# events. `signal died(tank)` declares one, `died.emit(self)` fires it, and `tank.died.connect(_on_enemy_died)` subscribes to it.
- **`await`** works like C# `await`. `var id = await screen.picked` waits for the player to pick a card, and `await get_tree().create_timer(1.5).timeout` waits 1.5 s.
- **Autoloads** are global singletons that survive scene changes.
- **Collision layers and masks:** a layer is "what I am", and a mask is "what I bump into".
- **You can't add physics objects inside a collision callback.** Use `call_deferred()` instead, as `main.gd` does when dropping power-ups.
- **`user://`** is the game's own save folder. `ConfigFile` saves settings, and `HTTPRequest` downloads updates.

This project builds everything in code so it reads top to bottom. The next Godot skill to try is building a tank as a **scene** in the editor (`CharacterBody2D` + `Sprite2D` + `CollisionShape2D`), then `preload()` and `instantiate()` it. Another is using a `TileMapLayer` for walls.
