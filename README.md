# Tank Siege

A modern take on the classic top-down tank game, built in **Godot 4.5** with **GDScript**.
Everything is original: the pixel art, sound effects and chiptune music are all generated by small Python scripts in `tools/`, so you can tweak and regenerate them.

**Features:** **local 2-player co-op**, 3 difficulty levels, special tiles (ice, mud, conveyor belts, teleporters, explosive barrels), a title screen with saved high scores, 3 stages that loop, **two bosses** (every 5th stage), 8 enemy types with pathfinding AI, a **combo multiplier** with hit-stop, 6 power-ups, 10 roguelite upgrade cards, a **Garage** with unlockable tanks and permanent perks, a level editor, secret codes, options, and an update checker with auto-update.

## Run it

1. Install Godot 4.5 or newer (the standard version, not .NET) from godotengine.org.
2. Open Godot. In the Project Manager, click **Import**, pick `project.godot` from this folder, then click **Import & Edit**.
3. Press **F5** (or the ▶ button, top right). The game starts at the title screen.

## Pause menu and options

Press **P** or **Esc** to open the pause menu: **Resume**, **Options**, **Restart** and **Main menu**. The same Options page is on the title screen. It has:
- volume sliders for **Master**, **Music** and **Sound effects**
- an on/off switch for **Screen shake**
- **Resolution**: every window size that fits your screen (512×416, 768×624, 1024×832, 1280×1040…), or **Fullscreen**
- **Pixel-perfect scaling**: only scales by whole numbers (2×, 3×…), so every pixel is exactly the same size. It adds black borders when the window doesn't divide evenly.
- an on/off switch for **Check for updates**

About resolution: the game is always *drawn* on a 512×416 canvas (`display/window/size/viewport_*` in `project.godot`), because all the UI and pixel art is laid out on that grid. Godot then scales that canvas up to the window (`stretch/mode = canvas_items`), so text and shapes are rendered sharply at the real window size. `Settings._apply_display()` sets the window size and scaling mode.

Everything works with the mouse, the keyboard (arrows + Enter) or a gamepad. Settings are saved when you leave the Options page. They're stored in `user://settings.cfg`, which you can find with **Project → Open User Data Folder** in the editor, and they're loaded again next time you play.

How it's built:
- `settings.gd` is an autoload that stores the options, applies them to the audio buses and window, and saves them with `ConfigFile`.
- `menu_kit.gd` has small helpers that build menus from `Button`, `HSlider` and `CheckButton` nodes and connect their signals (`pressed`, `value_changed`, `toggled`). It also builds the shared Options page. The title screen, pause menu and level editor all use it.
- `ui_theme.gd` builds a `Theme` in code. It's the UI's "style sheet": set it on one node and all its children inherit the look.

## The rules

Protect the glowing core at the bottom. Each stage has 20 enemies, with at most 4 on screen at once. Clear them all to advance. If a shell hits the core, or you run out of lives, it's game over.

**Enemy types:**

| Tank | Color | From stage | What it does |
|------|-------|-----------|--------------|
| Basic | grey | 1 | The classic |
| Fast | mint | 1 | Quick, and hunts you |
| Power | purple | 1 | Fires fast shells |
| Armored | green | 1 | Takes 4 hits (glows hotter as it's damaged) |
| **Kamikaze** | orange | 2 | Never shoots. Rushes you, lights a fuse, and **explodes**, hurting everything nearby, including other enemies. It also blows up walls that block it. Shoot it early, ideally next to its friends! |
| **Sniper** | navy | 3 | When it lines up with you, it stops, shows a **red laser sight** (your warning), then fires a very fast shell. Break the line or dodge. |
| **Shielded** | teal | 4 | A steel plate **deflects shells from the front**. Hit it from the side or behind. Explosions ignore the shield. |
| **Engineer** | khaki | 4 | Drives to bricks you've shot away and **rebuilds** them (2 at a time). Kill it first. |
Enemies #4, #11 and #18 blink red. Destroy one to drop a power-up. It always lands on an empty block you can drive to: never inside walls or water, under a bush, on a belt or teleporter, or somewhere walled off by steel. (Bricks in the way are fine, because you can shoot through them.) See `Level.powerup_spots()`.

| Icon | Power-up | Effect |
|------|----------|--------|
| Star | Power | Upgrade your tank: faster shells, then 2 shells at once, then shells that break steel. **Your tank changes shape at every level**, like the original: long gun, then twin guns, then heavy armor. You lose these when you die (except on Easy). |
| Shield | Shield | 10 seconds of invulnerability |
| Snowflake | Freeze | Enemies stop for 8 seconds |
| Bomb | Bomb | Destroys every enemy on screen |
| Bricks | Walls | The core's walls turn to steel for 15 seconds |
| Heart | Life | One extra life |

## Combo multiplier and hit-stop

Kills less than **2.5 seconds** apart build a **combo**. Each kill in the chain is worth its points × the chain length (up to **×8**).
- Score popups float up from every kill.
- The combo sound climbs in pitch with each link.
- The sidebar shows the multiplier and a bar that drains until the chain breaks.
- Chains of 3, 5, 8 and 12 get a shout: NICE!, GREAT!, UNSTOPPABLE!, LEGENDARY!

Every kill also triggers a tiny **hit-stop**: the game drops to 5% speed for about 50–100 ms. It's barely visible, but it makes hits feel heavy. Fighting games do the same. See `_register_kill()` and `hit_stop()` in `main.gd`. `Engine.time_scale` slows the whole game, and the freeze ends on the real clock, because `delta` itself is slowed down.

## Bosses (every 5th stage)

The two bosses take turns: the **Fortress** on stages 5, 15, 25…, and the **Sky Raider** gunship on stages 10, 20, 30…. Each gets tougher every time you meet it ("MK-2", "MK-3"…).

### Sky Raider (stages 10, 20…)
A helicopter gunship that **flies**: it passes over walls and water, casts a shadow, and you can't block it. It hovers above your column firing aimed 3-shell bursts from its wing pods. Every few seconds it flashes a **red warning stripe** across one row, then makes a **strafing run** along it, raining shells that chew through bricks. Get out of the stripe! Its runs come more often as it takes damage. At a third of its health it enrages: it glows red, gets faster, fires rings of 8 shells and calls in escorts. It's `gunship.gd`, which extends `Boss` and replaces only the movement, the attacks and the drawing. It lives on its own physics layer, `AIR`, so only shells touch it.

### Fortress (stages 5, 15…)
Stages 5, 15… (see above) are Fortress stages. A siren sounds, the **Fortress** (a 64×64 twin-cannon tank) rolls in with its own music and a health bar, and 8 escorts come with it. It has three phases:

| Health | Phase |
|--------|-------|
| 100–66% | Patrols the top, fires a 3-way spread |
| 66–33% | Adds shots aimed at you, and calls in escort tanks |
| 33–0% | **Enraged**: glows red, moves lower and faster, fires rings of 8 shells |

It ignores Freeze, and a Bomb only damages it. It gets 15 more HP every time you meet it. Destroying it sets off a chain of explosions, drops 2 power-ups and clears the stage. The boss arena (`BOSS_MAP` in `level_data.gd`) has a steel bunker above your core, so its straight-down shots can't hit the core directly. The boss is `boss.gd`.

Every run pays out **scrap** when it ends:
- score ÷ 100
- \+ 10 per stage reached
- \+ 2 per best-combo kill
- \+ 25 per boss

Custom levels and runs with cheats don't pay. Spend scrap in **Title → Garage**:

| Tank | Cost | Style |
|------|------|-------|
| Scout | free | Balanced |
| Striker | 60 | +1 shell on screen, faster shells and reload, slightly slower |
| Bulwark | 80 | Survives an extra hit every life, but slow |
| Phantom | 120 | Very fast, shells pierce one tank, one fewer life |
| Demolisher | 150 | Every shell explodes, slow reload |

| Perk | Cost | Effect |
|------|------|--------|
| Spare Parts | 40 / 90 | +1 starting life (2 levels) |
| Head Start | 50 | Start every run with a free common upgrade card |
| Reroll | 60 | Reroll the upgrade cards once per stage |
| Wide Choice | 100 | Choose from 4 cards instead of 3 |
| Scrap Magnet | 50 / 110 | +25% scrap (2 levels) |

A gold arrow on the back always marks *your* tank, whatever its color. Everything is saved in `user://save.cfg`.

**Add your own tank or perk:** it's all data in `garage.gd`. A tank is a dictionary of stat changes (`speed`, `extra_shells`, `armor`, `pierce`, `blast`, `reload`…). For its color, add a palette line in `tools/make_sprites.py` and rerun it. A perk is a name, a list of prices, and a `GameState.perk("id")` check wherever it should do something.

## Co-op (2 players)

**Title → Co-op (2 players)**, then pick a difficulty. Both players share one screen, keyboard, or two gamepads.

| | Player 1 (yellow) | Player 2 (green) |
|---|---|---|
| Move | W A S D | Arrow keys |
| Fire | Space (or J) | Enter, Right Ctrl, or Numpad 0 |
| Gamepad | first gamepad | second gamepad |
| Spawns | left of the core | right of the core |

- Each player has **their own lives and star level** (the tank shape shows it). Stars, shields and extra lives go to whoever picks them up.
- **Score, combo and upgrade cards are shared.** It's a team.
- If one player runs out of lives, the other fights on. It's game over when both are out, or when the core falls.
- Enemies go after whichever player is **closest**, and one extra enemy is allowed on screen.
- Player 1 drives the tank picked in the Garage; Player 2 drives a green Scout.

How it works: `Player.new(index)` builds each tank with its own set of input actions (`p1_up`, `p2_fire`, ... in `game_state.gd`). Each gamepad event is tied to a device number, so gamepad 1 only moves Player 1. `GameState.lives_p` and `stars_p` hold one value per player.

## Tiles

| Tile | Map char | What it does |
|------|----------|--------------|
| Brick | `B` | Breakable wall |
| Steel | `S` | Only max-power shells break it |
| Water | `W` | Blocks tanks, shells fly over |
| Bush | `G` | Hides tanks and shells |
| **Ice** | `I` | Slippery: you keep sliding after letting go, and turns are sluggish |
| **Mud** | `M` | Half speed. The AI avoids it when there's another way. |
| **Conveyor belt** | `^` `V` `<` `>` | Pushes any tank that way, even one standing still |
| **Teleporter** | `T` | Drive onto the middle of a pad and come out of the next pad, in map order. Enemies can use them too! A pad blocked by a tank is skipped. |
| **Explosive barrel** | `X` | Solid. Shoot it (or blast it) and it explodes, destroying nearby bricks and damaging every tank and the core. Barrels close together go off in a **chain reaction**. |

The new **Stage 4 "Frozen Factory"** shows them all off. Stages now loop through 4 maps, with the boss arena every 5th stage.

How they work:
- Ice, mud, belts and pads are **floor tiles**. They have no collision. `Level.floor_under()` tells a tank what it's standing on, and `Tank.drive()` applies it: mud scales speed, belts add a push, and ice uses `move_toward()` so your speed changes slowly (tweak `ICE_GRIP`, `MUD_SPEED` and `CONVEYOR_SPEED` in `tank.gd`).
- `floor_layer.gd` draws all floor tiles in one node, under the walls and tanks.
- Teleporting is `Level.try_teleport()`, with a short cooldown so you don't bounce straight back.
- Barrels are walls of kind `BARREL`. When one is hit, `Level.explode_barrel()` removes it and, a moment later, damages everything in `BARREL_RADIUS`. That damage can hit other barrels, which is where the chain reactions come from.

## Difficulty

After **Play**, pick **Easy**, **Normal** or **Hard**. The game remembers your last choice, and each difficulty has its own high score.

| | Easy | Normal | Hard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starting lives | +2 | 3 | −1 |
| Enemy speed / shell speed | ×0.85 / ×0.8 | ×1 | ×1.15 / ×1.2 |
| Enemy fire rate | slower | normal | faster |
| Enemies on screen at once | 3 | 4 | 5 |
| Time between spawns | 3.0 s | 2.5 s | 2.0 s |
| Smart AI waits before hunting | +4 s | normal | −1.5 s |
| Enemy reaction / turn / aim time | ×1.5 | ×1 | ×0.7 |
| Boss health | ×0.75 | ×1 | ×1.35 |
| Star level after dying | **kept** | lost | lost |
| Scrap earned | ×0.75 | ×1 | ×1.5 |

Every number is in one table in `scripts/difficulty.gd`, so you can rebalance by editing it. Any script reads a value with `GameState.diff("enemy_speed")`.

## Star levels change your tank

As in the original, each star you collect changes your tank's **shape**, not just its stats. Every star also bolts on **one more armor plate**: one extra hit you can survive.

| Stars | Look | Effect | Hits to destroy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | light tank, short gun | normal | 1 |
| 1 | long gun with a muzzle brake | faster shells | 2 |
| 2 | bigger turret, twin guns | 2 shells on screen | 3 |
| 3 | heavy tank, armor plates, twin heavy guns | shells break steel | 4 |

The small grey plates on your treads show your armor. A plate that has been shot off turns dark. After a hit you blink for a second, so you can get away. A star picked up at level 3 **repairs one lost plate**. Armor from stars adds to the Armor Plating card and the Garage tanks' armor. The numbers are in `Player._armor_total()` (`ARMOR_PER_STAR`).

All 5 Garage tanks have all 4 shapes (`assets/sprites/tank_<color>_L0..L3.png`). They're drawn in `tools/make_sprites.py` from a few numbers per level (`LEVEL_SHAPES`): gun length, turret size, number of barrels and armor plates. The game picks the sprite in `Player._current_sprite()`.

## Game over

When the game ends, whether you ran out of lives or the core fell, **everything stops where it is**. Tanks freeze, and shells in flight fizzle out. Explosions and score popups still finish playing. See `_freeze_battlefield()` in `main.gd`: switching off `_physics_process` on every tank freezes its movement, AI and shooting in one go. After a moment, the stats screen appears with **Retry** and **Main menu** buttons (Esc also goes to the menu). It has two tabs: **This stage** (the stage you fell on, shown first) and **Whole run** (every stage added up).

## Stats screen

After every stage there's a **stats screen**, and at game over there's one for the whole run:

- **DESTROYED**: a tally like the original game. There's one row per enemy type, and the counter blips up one tank at a time. In co-op there's a column for each player.
- **The numbers**: time, shells fired, accuracy, best combo, wall pieces smashed, power-ups, enemy shells shot down, armor lost and score. After a stage these are for that stage only. At game over there are two tabs: **This stage** and **Whole run** (which adds stages cleared), plus a new high score and the scrap you earned.
- **AWARDS** (stage only): small score bonuses for playing well.

| Award | For | Bonus |
|---|---|---|
| No Scratch | taking no damage | +1000 |
| Sharpshooter | 75%+ of your shells hitting | +500 |
| Blitz | clearing the stage in under 1:30 | +500 |
| Combo King | a x5 combo or better | +500 |
| Wrecking Ball | 40+ wall pieces smashed | +300 |
| Hoarder | 3+ power-ups | +300 |
| MVP P1/P2 | co-op: most tanks destroyed | +1000 |

- **ACHIEVEMENTS**: any you unlocked during the stage or run.

Press **Fire / Enter** once to skip the animation, then again to continue to the upgrade cards. At game over, choose **Retry** or **Main menu**.

**How it works:** `GameState.track("shots", 1, player_index)` counts things as they happen: in `bullet.gd` (hits, walls, shells shot down), `player.gd` (shots, armor lost), `main.gd` (kills, deaths, power-ups) and `level.gd` (teleports). Every counter goes into three dictionaries:
- `stage_stats`, for this stage's screen
- `run_stats`, for the game-over screen
- `lifetime`, which is saved and used by achievements

Kills know who made them because each shell sets `tank.last_hit_by` to its player index before it hits. Awards are in `Main._stage_medals()`, and the screen itself is `stats_screen.gd`.

## Achievements

There are 33 achievements, in bronze, silver and gold. When you unlock one, an "ACHIEVEMENT UNLOCKED" popup slides in. Open **Achievements** on the title screen to see them all, with progress for the counting ones ("37 / 100"). A few are **secret**, and they show as "SECRET" until you find them.

A sample: *First Blood*, *Tank Buster* (100 kills), *Scrapyard Legend* (1,000), *LEGENDARY!* (a 12-kill combo), *Bomb Squad* (4 tanks with one Bomb), *Eagle Eye* (destroy a Sniper while its laser is on you), *Hot Potato* (shoot a lit Kamikaze), *Untouchable*, *Sharpshooter*, *Ammo Miser* (clear a stage with fewer than 40 shells), *Tiptoe* (clear a stage without breaking a single wall), *Last Stand*, *Brothers in Arms* (co-op), *Hard Boiled* (stage 5 on Hard), *Frequent Flyer* (25 teleports), *Never Give Up* (lose 50 tanks)...

Runs with **cheats don't unlock achievements or count toward the totals**, except for a couple of secret ones that are about cheating. Custom levels only give *Architect*, so a tiny homemade map can't farm the stage feats.

**Add your own:** add a line to `LIST` in `achievements.gd`. For a counter, give it a `"stat"` and a `"goal"` (any `GameState.track()` key). For anything else, call `Achievements.unlock("your_id")` where it happens. The popup and the title screen list pick it up automatically. Unlocked achievements and lifetime totals are saved in `user://save.cfg`.

## Title screen and high score

The title screen has **Play**, **Co-op**, **Garage**, **Achievements**, **Custom levels**, **Level editor**, **Options** and **Quit**, in two columns. Under the menu are your records: the high score and best stage on your last difficulty, your scrap and your achievements. The difficulty page shows the record for whichever difficulty you point at. ## Smarter enemies (pathfinding AI)

Each enemy gets a **brain** when it spawns:

| Brain | Who gets it | What it does |
|-------|-------------|--------------|
| **Wander** | most basic tanks | The classic: drives around, turns at random, drifts toward the base |
| **Raider** | armored tanks, some power and basic tanks | Plans a route to a spot next to your core with **A\* pathfinding**, shoots through bricks on the way, then attacks the core |
| **Hunter** | fast tanks, some power and basic tanks | Plans a route to **you** and re-plans as you move. When it lines up with you in a row or column (with no steel in between) it stops, turns and fires |

Every brain also fires sooner when you or the core are straight ahead.

**Enemies need time to react.** An enemy can't spin round and shoot you the moment you line up with it. Here is what happens first (times are on Normal):

1. It **notices** you (0.3 s).
2. It **turns** on the spot, and you can see it rotate: 0.25 s for a quarter turn, 0.45 s to turn right round.
3. It **steadies the gun** (0.35 s after any turn, including turns made while driving).

So a tank facing away from you needs about **1.1 s** before it can fire. That's enough time to drive up behind it and shoot first. While it's getting ready, the **tip of its barrel glows orange**. That's your cue to shoot or dodge. Easy gives you 1.5× that time and Hard 0.7×. The constants are at the top of `enemy.gd` (`REACTION`, `TURN_90`, `TURN_180`, `AIM_TIME`). The Sniper's laser countdown also starts only after it has turned toward you. To keep it fair, smart tanks wander for a few seconds first (raiders longer on early stages), and only 1 raider is allowed at a time on stages 1–2. That rises to 2 on stages 3–5 and 3 after that. Later stages have more smart tanks.

**How it works:** `level.gd` builds an `AStarGrid2D`, Godot's built-in A\* for grids, with one point for every place a tank can stand. Steel, water and the core are **solid**. Bricks are walkable but **expensive** (`BRICK_COST`), so raiders prefer open roads but will blast through a wall when it's much shorter. When a wall is destroyed, only the few points around it are updated. `enemy.gd` follows the path point by point. Press **F3** while testing to *see* the planned routes: yellow for raiders, red for hunters.

## Pixel-art sprites

All the art is in `assets/sprites/`: 6 tank colors with 2 tread frames each, brick, steel, animated water, bushes, the core (intact and destroyed), the power-up icons and the app icon. The art is small (tanks are 16×16 pixels) and shown at 2×. The game uses **Nearest** texture filtering (`texture_filter = TEXTURE_FILTER_NEAREST`), so the pixels stay sharp instead of blurry.

- **Edit the art** in any pixel editor (Aseprite, LibreSprite, Piskel) and save over the PNGs. Keep the sizes the same.
- **Or edit the text grids** in `tools/make_sprites.py`, where every character is one pixel. Then run `python tools/make_sprites.py` (needs Python 3 + Pillow). This regenerates *all* sprites, so don't run it after hand-editing PNGs.
- `art.gd` loads textures and draws single frames from sprite sheets. Tanks draw their sprite rotated to face their direction and swap between the 2 frames as they move, so the treads roll. Tints change the look without new art: frozen tanks turn blue, damaged armor glows, and the hit flash uses white.

## Building the game (Windows / macOS)

**Automatically on GitHub (easiest).** The repo includes a build script, `.github/workflows/build.yml`. To publish a version:
1. Bump the version in `scripts/version.gd` **and** `project.godot` (`config/version`). They must match.
2. Commit, then tag and push:
   ```
   git tag v1.5.1
   git push origin main v1.5.1
   ```
3. Wait a minute or two. A new release appears on the repo's **Releases** page with:
   - `TankSiege-windows.zip`, a single `.exe`
   - `TankSiege-macos.zip`, a universal `.app` for Apple Silicon and Intel

   The file names never change, so `https://github.com/<you>/tank-siege/releases/latest/download/TankSiege-windows.zip` always gets the newest version. The website in `site/` uses these links.
   - `tank-siege.pck` and `update.json`, for the auto-updater

To just try a build without a release, open the **Actions** tab → **Build game** → **Run workflow**. The zips appear under **Artifacts** on that run's page. If the tag doesn't match the game version, the build stops with a clear error, so a release can never ship with the wrong number.

It all runs on a Linux machine, because Godot can export Windows and Mac games from Linux. For a private repo, GitHub gives 2,000 free build minutes a month, and one build takes 1–2.

**By hand, in the Godot editor:**
1. Go to **Editor → Manage Export Templates → Download and Install** (once).
2. Go to **Project → Export**. The *Windows Desktop* and *macOS* presets are already set up in `export_presets.cfg`.
3. Pick one and click **Export Project…**. Keep **Export With Debug** off for a release: it's faster and turns off the F2/F3 test keys.

**Unsigned-app warnings.** The builds aren't signed with a paid certificate, so the first launch shows a warning:
- **Windows** ("Windows protected your PC"): click **More info → Run anyway**.
- **macOS**: right-click the app → **Open** → **Open**. If it says the app "is damaged", run `xattr -cr "/Applications/Tank Siege.app"`.

To get rid of the macOS warning, you need an Apple Developer account ($99/year). Put your certificate and notarization details in the macOS preset's *Codesign* and *Notarization* options.

**Settings that matter:**
- Windows: *Embed PCK* is on, so you get one `.exe` file.
- macOS: universal architecture and bundle id `com.foxsoft2005.tanksiege`.
- `project.godot` turns on *Import ETC2 ASTC*, which Godot requires for Apple Silicon builds.

## Website

`site/` is a one-page website for the game: `index.html` plus the images in `site/img/`, with no build step. It uses the game's own palette, sprites and screenshots. Its two download buttons point to
`https://github.com/foxsoft2005/tank-siege/releases/latest/download/TankSiege-windows.zip` (and `-macos.zip`), so after each new Release the page gets the newest version with no edits. The button for the visitor's own computer is marked automatically.

**Hosting:** upload the `site/` folder anywhere that serves static files: GitHub Pages, Netlify, Cloudflare Pages, or itch.io as an HTML page. The download links only work for visitors when the release files are public. For that, make this repository public, or publish the zips somewhere public (a second public repo, or itch.io) and change the two `href`s in `index.html`.

To change the screenshots, replace the PNGs in `site/img/`. The page shows them at 512×416, the game's own resolution.

## Updates and auto-update

The game can check the internet for a newer version and install it. It's **off until you set it up**, because it needs somewhere to host your files. Two good options:
- **GitHub:** host `update.json` in the repo and attach the `.pck` to a release.
- **itch.io:** host the game there, and put `update.json` plus the `.pck` on any HTTPS host.

**What the player sees:** the title screen shows "Update v1.1.0 available!" with **Download & install** and **Open download page**. There's a progress bar while it downloads, then "Restart now". After the restart, the new version is running.

**How it works** (`scripts/updater.gd`):
1. It downloads a small `update.json` "manifest" from `MANIFEST_URL`.
2. It compares versions number by number, so 1.10.0 counts as newer than 1.9.0.
3. It downloads your game's `.pck` (a Godot *resource pack* containing all game files). It checks the file's **SHA-256 fingerprint** against the manifest and throws away anything damaged or modified.
4. It saves the pack to `user://updates/`. On the next launch, the `Updater` autoload is created *first* and mounts that pack before anything else loads, so the whole game runs the new version.

Safety details: only `https://` URLs are allowed. The patch is re-checked on every launch. A patch older than the installed game is deleted automatically. Deleting `user://updates/` rolls back.

**Publishing an update, step by step:**
1. Make your changes and bump `VERSION` in `scripts/version.gd`, for example `1.0.0` → `1.1.0`. Also update *Project Settings → Application → Config → Version*.
2. Install the export templates (**Editor → Manage Export Templates**). The Windows and macOS presets are already in **Project → Export**. The GitHub build script also makes the `.pck` and `update.json` for you: see "Building the game".
3. In the Export window, click **Export PCK/ZIP…** and save `tank-siege.pck`.
4. Upload the `.pck`, for example as a GitHub release asset, and copy its download URL.
5. Run `python tools/make_update_manifest.py tank-siege.pck 1.1.0 <pck URL> <your game page URL> "What's new"`. It writes `update.json`.
6. Upload `update.json` to the address you put in `MANIFEST_URL`. Everyone running the game now gets the update.

For the very first release, set `MANIFEST_URL` in `updater.gd` (for example `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/you/tank-siege/main/update.json`) and export the full game normally.

**Limits:** a patch can change any scene, script, art, sound or music. It can't change `project.godot` (window size, the autoload list, input settings) or add *new* `class_name` scripts. For changes like those, publish a full new download and set `page_url`, so players get the "Open download page" button.

**Testing updates on your computer:** patches are ignored when you run from the editor, so a downloaded patch can't hide your source files. To test the whole flow:
1. Serve a folder with `python -m http.server 8765`.
2. Put a `.pck` and `update.json` in that folder, using `http://localhost:8765/...` URLs.
3. Launch with `godot --path . -- --test-updates --manifest=http://localhost:8765/update.json`.

## Upgrade cards (roguelite)

After every stage, the game pauses and offers **3 random upgrade cards**. Pick one. Upgrades last for the whole run and are lost on game over. Rarer cards show up less often. Unlike the P power-up, you keep upgrade cards when you die.

| Card | Rarity | Max | Effect |
|------|--------|-----|--------|
| Swift Treads | Common | 3 | +15% movement speed |
| Rapid Loader | Common | 2 | +1 shell on screen at once |
| Armor Plating | Common | 2 | Survive one more hit per life (grey plates on your treads) |
| Field Repair | Common | ∞ | +1 life |
| Ricochet | Rare | 2 | Shells bounce sideways off walls |
| Piercing Rounds | Rare | 2 | Shells pass through enemy tanks |
| Blast Shells | Rare | 1 | Shells explode, hurting nearby enemies and breaking bricks |
| Core Shield | Rare | 2 | The core survives extra hits every stage |
| Twin Cannon | Epic | 1 | Two shells side by side |
| Guard Drone | Epic | 2 | Drones orbit you and shoot nearby enemies |

**Add your own upgrade in 2 steps:**
1. Add an entry to `ALL` in `scripts/upgrades.gd`. It shows up as a card automatically.
2. Read its level wherever it should matter with `GameState.stacks("your_id")`. For example, `player.gd` does `speed = BASE_SPEED * (1.0 + 0.15 * GameState.stacks("swift_treads"))`.

Press **F2** while testing to skip straight to the card screen.

## Sound

All sound effects live in `assets/sfx/` as `.wav` files, and one autoload, `scripts/sfx.gd`, plays them. From any script, call:

```gdscript
Sfx.play("shoot")               # normal
Sfx.play("enemy_shoot", -4.0)   # 4 dB quieter
Sfx.play("stage_clear", 0, 0)   # no random pitch variation
```

`Sfx` keeps a small pool of `AudioStreamPlayer` nodes so sounds can overlap. It adds a little random pitch to each play, which makes repeated shots feel less robotic. It also skips a sound if the same one started less than 40 ms ago, so four explosions from a Bomb don't stack into one ear-splitting blast.

**Change a sound:** replace the `.wav` in `assets/sfx/` with your own, keeping the same file name. Good free tools are [jsfxr](https://sfxr.me) and ChipTone. **Add a sound:** drop the file in, add its name to `SOUND_NAMES` in `sfx.gd`, then call `Sfx.play("your_name")`.

**Tweak the generated sounds:** edit a recipe in `tools/make_sfx.py`, then run `python tools/make_sfx.py` (needs Python 3 + numpy). Each sound is an oscillator (square, triangle, sine or noise) with a pitch sweep and a volume envelope, the same idea sfxr uses. Godot re-imports the files automatically.

## Music

Three original chiptune loops live in `assets/music/`:

| Track | Plays during | Style |
|-------|--------------|-------|
| `battle_a.ogg` | odd stages (1, 3, 5...) | A minor, 150 BPM |
| `battle_b.ogg` | even stages | D minor, 160 BPM |
| `upgrade.ogg`  | the upgrade card screen | calm, no drums, 96 BPM |

The `Music` autoload (`scripts/music.gd`) handles them:

```gdscript
Music.play("battle_a")     # fades over from whatever was playing
Music.stop()               # fades out
Music.set_muffled(true)    # low-pass "underwater" filter (used when paused)
```

Because it's an autoload, the music keeps playing smoothly when a stage reloads. It also sets up two **audio buses**, `Music` and `SFX`, both feeding into `Master`. That's why N can silence just the music, and why the pause filter only affects the music. To make the music louder or quieter, change `VOLUME_DB` in `music.gd`.

**Use your own music:** drop an `.ogg` into `assets/music/` with one of the names above. Free chiptune music is on OpenGameArt.org (check each track's license), or you can make your own in a free tracker like FamiStudio or BeepBox.

**Edit the generated songs:** `tools/make_music.py` holds the songs as plain lists of chords and `(note, length)` pairs, like a tiny tracker. Change a melody and run `python tools/make_music.py` (needs Python 3, numpy and ffmpeg). The script explains how each "chip channel" (pulse lead, arpeggio, triangle bass, noise drums) is built.

## Secret codes (spoilers!)

Type these words on the keyboard during a stage, or in the pause menu. You don't need to type them into anything. A sparkle sound and a message confirm the code worked.

| Code | Effect |
|------|--------|
| `GODTIER` | **God mode** on/off. Nothing can destroy your tank (golden halo). Your core can still fall! |
| ↑ ↑ ↓ ↓ ← → ← → B A | **+30 lives.** A tribute to the classic Konami code. Works on a gamepad too (D-pad, then B and A). |
| `OVERLOAD` | Max power (P level 3: fast shells, 2 at once, breaks steel) |
| `BLASTOFF` | Destroy every enemy on screen |
| `CASTLE` | Steel walls around the core for the rest of the stage, plus 3 core shield charges |
| `SHORTCUT` | Skip the stage (you still get your upgrade card) |
| `LOOTBOX` | 3 free random upgrades |
| `DISCO` | Rainbow tanks on/off. Purely for fun. |

God mode and disco stay on through a restart until you type the code again. The game-over screen admits it if you used cheats.

The letters are read by **key position** (QWERTY), so the codes also work with a Russian or Estonian layout active. While typing, W/A/S/D will nudge your tank a little. Open the pause menu first if you'd rather not move.

**How it works / add your own:** `cheat_codes.gd` remembers the last 16 keys and gamepad buttons you pressed. It checks whether that history *ends with* one of the codes in its `CODES` list. If so, it emits `code_entered("id")`, and `Main._on_cheat()` decides what happens. To add a code, add a line to `CODES` and a `match` branch in `_on_cheat()`. Avoid the letters P, M, N and J in word codes, because those keys already do something (pause, mute, music, fire).

### Godot ideas, translated for a C# / Python programmer

- **Nodes and the scene tree.** A game is a tree of nodes. Each node type does one job: `Node2D` has a position, `CharacterBody2D` is something you move that collides, `Area2D` detects overlaps, and `Label` shows text. Adding a node with `add_child()` puts it in the game. Removing it with `queue_free()` deletes it at the end of the frame.
- **Scripts extend node types.** `extends CharacterBody2D` is like `class Tank : CharacterBody2D`. `class_name Tank` makes it available everywhere by name. `Player extends Tank` is normal inheritance, and `super(delta)` calls the parent method.
- **Overridable hooks.** `Enemy` calls functions like `_think()`, `_configure()` and `_path_goal()` that subclasses override. That's how `KamikazeEnemy` or `EngineerEnemy` change behaviour in ~50 lines each without touching the base class. `Enemy.create(kind)` is a small *factory* that builds the right subclass.
- **Engine callbacks** (like Unity's `Update`):
  - `_ready()` runs once, when the node enters the game.
  - `_process(delta)` runs every frame.
  - `_physics_process(delta)` runs at a fixed 60 Hz. Use it for movement.
  - `_draw()` is where you draw shapes. Call `queue_redraw()` to request a redraw.
- **Signals** are C# events. `signal died(tank)` declares one, `died.emit(self)` fires it, and `tank.died.connect(_on_enemy_died)` subscribes to it.
- **`await`** works like C# `async`/`await`. In `main.gd`, `var id = await screen.picked` pauses that function until the player clicks a card. `await get_tree().create_timer(1.5).timeout` waits 1.5 seconds.
- **UI** uses `Control` nodes (`Label`, `Button`, `HBoxContainer`...). Containers lay out their children automatically. Buttons get mouse, keyboard and gamepad support through Godot's focus system.
- **Physics callbacks.** You can't add physics objects (bodies, areas) inside a collision callback. Use `call_deferred()` instead, as `main.gd` does when dropping power-ups.
- **Collision layers and masks.** A layer is "what I am" and a mask is "what I bump into". Water sits on its own layer so tanks hit it but bullets fly over. See `layers.gd`.
- **Autoload.** `Updater`, `GameState`, `Music`, `Sfx` and `Settings` are global singletons that persist when `reload_current_scene()` restarts the stage. That's also why sounds don't cut off when a stage reloads.
- **Pause.** `get_tree().paused = true` freezes every node whose `process_mode` is pausable. `Main` is set to "always" so it can still unpause.

- **Scenes.** `change_scene_to_file()` swaps the whole scene, for example from title to game to editor. Autoloads survive the swap.
- **Files.** `user://` is the game's own save folder. `ConfigFile` saves settings and the high score, `FileAccess` and `DirAccess` handle level files, and `HTTPRequest` downloads updates.

This project builds everything in code so you can read it top to bottom. As you learn, try the Godot way: build a Tank **scene** in the editor (a `CharacterBody2D` with a `Sprite2D` and a `CollisionShape2D`), then `preload()` and `instantiate()` it. Also try a `TileMapLayer` for walls.

## Suggested next steps (roughly easy → harder)

1. **Tweak numbers.** Try `COMBO_WINDOW` and `MAX_MULTIPLIER` in `main.gd`, the boss HP in `boss.gd`, and the tank stats and prices in `garage.gd`.
2. **Make stages.** Build some in the level editor, use "Copy as code", and add them to `level_data.gd`.
3. **A new enemy.** Copy `enemies/shielded_enemy.gd`, give it a twist (a mine-layer, or a tank that splits in two when destroyed), add it to `Enemy.Kind`, `Enemy.create()` and the spawn table in `main.gd`.
4. **A second boss.** Alternate bosses every 5 stages, for example a fast hovercraft that dashes.
5. **Achievements.** Goals like "win a stage without firing" or "×8 combo", saved with `ConfigFile` and shown in the Garage.
6. **Local co-op.** Add a Player 2 with its own input actions (`p2_move_up`, and so on).
7. **Ship it.** Export for Windows and Web, publish on itch.io, and turn on the updater.
